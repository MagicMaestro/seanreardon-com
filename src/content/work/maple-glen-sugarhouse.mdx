A 2017 rebuild for a maple sugarhouse client — small, owner-operated, no internal web team. Their existing site was a desktop-era marketing page with no mobile layout and no way to take orders online; what they needed was both, plus a way to update product inventory themselves between tapping seasons.

The interesting constraint wasn't technical — it was operational. The owners would be running the webstore alone, between making syrup. They needed a CMS they could log into on a phone in February at 11pm and update without breaking anything. WordPress was the right answer here even though I'd built fancier stacks before — it had a webstore ecosystem (WooCommerce + a Square payments plugin) that the owners could realistically operate without me on call, and a theme architecture I could customize without pulling in plugins I didn't want.

The build was a custom WordPress theme — responsive grid layout, a featured-products slider on the homepage, a tour-information page, and the storefront with Square checkout. The Square integration was straightforward at the time; what took longer was tuning the editorial admin experience so adding a new syrup grade was three fields and a photo upload, not twelve.

The site is still up nearly a decade later, which is probably the strongest signal you can get for "right tool for the people who'd own it long-term." Not the most ambitious project I shipped that year, but the one I think about when a client asks about something more elaborate that they wouldn't be able to maintain.
